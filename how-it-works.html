Product overview

From conversation to learning insight.

FeedbackRx Coach structures the entire feedback loop so clinical educators and learners both leave with clarity, action, and a record that helps them grow.

The feedback loop

Five steps. One simple workflow.

1

Prepare the conversation

Learner and faculty set intent, choose a framework, and identify the behavior they want to discuss. The conversation starts with purpose instead of surprise.

Prep inputs
  • Choose framework
  • Identify behavior
  • Set learner intent
2

Have the feedback conversation

Use Ask-Tell-Ask, SBI, PEARLS, or ADAPT to keep the conversation focused, kind, and useful. Both people know the structure, so nothing feels arbitrary.

Frameworks
  • Ask-Tell-Ask
  • Situation-Behavior-Impact
  • PEARLS Debriefing
  • ADAPT Model
3

Document what matters

Key points, agreed actions, and follow-up dates are captured in the moment. No one has to reconstruct the conversation from memory later.

Auto-captured
  • Observed behaviors
  • Agreed actions
  • Follow-up date
4

Coach the coach

Faculty review their own conversations or receive peer coaching. Educators improve their feedback skills privately and continuously.

Coaching outputs
  • Self-review notes
  • Peer observation
  • Growth goals
5

See program insight

Program leaders get snapshots of feedback volume, learner progress, and faculty development — without digging through scattered forms.

Program views
  • Learner progress
  • Faculty growth
  • Competency snapshots
Built-in benefits

What changes once feedback becomes structured.

🎯

Clarity for learners

Students and residents leave with specific behaviors to practice, not vague advice to interpret.

📈

Growth for faculty

Educators get feedback on their own feedback, so teaching quality improves over time.

🛡️

Trust and safety

Consent, access controls, and clear retention policies protect sensitive clinical education conversations.

⏱️

Less administrative drag

Documentation happens during the conversation, cutting note-taking time for faculty and program staff.
